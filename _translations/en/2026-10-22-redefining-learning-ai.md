**Key Takeaway**

AI can improve outputs without producing a corresponding increase in what people can do after assistance is removed. That gap looks different depending on whether we define learning within an individual or within a human–AI system. Yet redefining the unit of analysis does not resolve every concern: AI can be wrong, and people still decide whether to use its answers. Learning in the AI era should encompass enduring understanding, the capability to coordinate effectively with AI, and the judgment to recognize its errors.

### When Does Successful AI-Assisted Work Count as Learning?

Salomon, Perkins, and Globerson's (1991) distinction between “effects with” and “effects of” technology provides a starting point, alongside seven other sources.

The essay brings together theoretical accounts of learning and extended cognition with a high-school field experiment, an adult reasoning experiment, and a retrospective medical study. These studies do not test competing definitions of learning. Connecting their results with those definitions is the interpretation offered here.
</div>

## 01 · Under a Classical Definition, the Gap Is Clear

Soderstrom and Bjork (2015) distinguish observable performance during acquisition from relatively durable changes supporting **long-term retention and transfer**. Good practice performance is not sufficient evidence of learning. This perspective examines what remains with the individual when support is withdrawn.

| Study | Design | Findings relevant to the distinction |
|---|---|---|
| Bastani et al. (2025) | Field experiment with approximately 1,000 high-school students. | Practice performance improved by 48% with GPT Base and 127% with GPT Tutor. GPT Base users then scored 17% below controls on unassisted tests; the negative effect was largely mitigated by GPT Tutor. |
| Fernandes et al. (2026) | Adult reasoning studies with 246 and 452 participants. | Study 1 AI users performed about three points above a historical non-AI benchmark but overestimated their own assisted performance by about four points. This measures calibration, not a later unassisted test. |
| Budzyń et al. (2025) | Before-and-after observational study across four endoscopy centers, including 1,443 unassisted colonoscopies. | Following routine exposure to AI-assisted examinations, adenoma detection in examinations without AI declined from 28.4% to 22.4% (p = .0089). |

The studies point to different aspects of the distinction. Unrestricted assistance did not ensure unassisted mathematical performance; the tutor's combined hints and teacher-prepared information largely reduced that problem. In the adult experiment, stronger assisted performance coexisted with overestimation. In the clinical study, unassisted detection was lower after AI became routine.

**Evidence boundaries:** The mathematics experiment took place in one school, and the tutor combined several elements. Fernandes et al.'s initial performance comparison used historical data. Budzyń et al. was observational, not randomized, and the authors conclude that exposure *might* reduce unassisted detection. Findings from mathematics, logical reasoning, and endoscopy should not be generalized directly to all work.

## 02 · Whose Capability Are We Measuring?

The gap is most apparent when learning means what remains inside an individual. Salomon and colleagues (1991) distinguished improved performance **with** a technology from changes remaining **of** its use after the technology is removed. They asked whether intelligence belongs to a person alone or can be a property of a person–machine system. Some of the evidence above concerns retained independent capability; the reasoning experiment instead examines monitoring of assisted performance.

Clark and Chalmers' (1998) **extended mind** argument takes the system perspective further. A notebook consistently used by a person with memory difficulties can function as part of that person's cognition. At work, we do not judge a financial professional solely by their ability to calculate without a spreadsheet. Removing AI from assessment can therefore resemble taking away a craftsperson's tools: part of the apparent gap may reflect what we choose to measure. Sfard (1998) likewise warns against treating either acquisition or participation as the only legitimate metaphor for learning. AI-era learning need not fit exclusively within the classical individual account.

## 03 · A New Definition Does Not Remove the Need for Judgment

The notebook in the extended-mind argument met demanding conditions: dependable availability, ready access, and information that could be accepted on the basis of prior endorsement. Generative AI does not straightforwardly satisfy those conditions. Access can change, plausible answers may be false, and outputs are often encountered for the first time. Automatic trust is risky.

Even when people and AI are treated as one system, **some capacity to supervise the tool must remain with people**. AI can be wrong, and users must check its outputs (Bastani et al., 2025). Improved performance without improved self-monitoring, as in Fernandes et al., highlights this concern. Without relevant foundational knowledge, a wrong answer may appear entirely convincing.

> **DrSong's View · Learning Theory**
>
> Redefining learning should not become an alibi for failed learning. Equally, defining learning only as unassisted capability overlooks the capability to work well with AI. I propose three layers of relatively durable change arising from human–AI experience: **retained understanding and skill**, which remain without AI; **coordination**, the capability to achieve better results together; and **judgment**, the capacity to verify outputs and decide what should be delegated. Retention and transfer remain relevant; the object of learning becomes broader. Judgment depends on retained understanding: people cannot reliably recognize errors in a domain they do not understand. The [Generative Cognitive Pause](/weekly-insight/2026-10-11-ai-paradox-hrd/) is a design proposal for developing these layers together. This is a conceptual definition, not a validated three-factor model.

> **DrSong's View · Organizational Learning**
>
> Song and Chermack (2008) connect accumulated individual learning with knowledge embedded in organizational culture. If capability resides only in the tool, an organization may possess outputs without possessing the knowledge behind them. A tool change or error then threatens that capability. Saving a brief record of important judgments and checked evidence alongside the final file allows subsequent colleagues to learn from the decision.

## 04 · Assess the Layers Separately

- **Retained capability:** After a delay, ask people to explain a conclusion and its reasoning in their own words with the AI screen closed. An initial view recorded before AI use provides a basis for comparison (Soderstrom & Bjork, 2015).
- **Coordination:** Examine how people use AI on an unfamiliar task, not only the finished result. Record what they changed, why, and which suggestions they discarded (Salomon et al., 1991).
- **Judgment:** Ask people to identify errors in AI outputs and compare predicted with actual performance (Fernandes et al., 2026).
- **Independent performance over time:** In roles where AI becomes routine, retain baseline information and periodically examine the quality of work without assistance (Budzyń et al., 2025).

### Insight Questions

1. Do our evaluations treat learning as what individuals retain, or as what people and AI accomplish together?
2. Do employees know enough to recognize when AI is wrong?
3. What capability would remain in our organization if AI were unavailable tomorrow?
