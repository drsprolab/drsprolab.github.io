**Key Takeaway**

AI-generated learning materials can be clear, accessible, and effortless to use. Satisfaction rises. Yet a frictionless experience may improve immediate performance without producing lasting learning. A highly rated course may leave people having learned less than a more demanding one. HRD cannot afford to overlook that possibility.
</div>

## AI Paradox 8

## 01 · Two Courses, Two Scorecards

Consider two hypothetical courses. Course A provides AI videos, concise summaries, and an instantly responsive chatbot. It earns a satisfaction rating of 4.9. Participants say it was accessible and useful, with immediate practical relevance. Three months later, little has changed in how they work.

Course B scores 3.8. Participants spend a long time discussing cases and find some questions uncomfortable. Three months later, their teams have begun adding reasoning to reports and formally inviting opposing views in meetings. Which course appears in the annual HRD report as a success? Course A.

## 02 · Distinguish Satisfaction from Outcomes

Brinkerhoff's (2003) **Success Case Method** looks beyond averages. It identifies people who changed substantially after training and people who changed very little, then asks what enabled or obstructed that change. Value is sought in actual workplace experiences rather than an average satisfaction score.

Kirkpatrick's four levels distinguish reaction, learning, behavior, and results (Kirkpatrick & Kirkpatrick, 2016). They answer different questions; progress at one level does not automatically produce progress at the next. Positive reactions immediately after training cannot establish whether people learned or changed their behavior at work (Salas et al., 2012).

## 03 · AI Makes Smooth, Satisfying Training Easier to Produce

Learning science distinguishes immediate **performance** from durable **learning**. Ease and fluency can support the task at hand, while appropriate difficulty that requires thinking can help learning last (Soderstrom & Bjork, 2015). AI may make training easier, but an increase in satisfaction does not establish that understanding has deepened.

Transfer also depends heavily on workplace conditions, including supervisory support and opportunities to apply learning (Blume et al., 2010). Training effectiveness reflects the interplay of individual engagement with team and organizational conditions (Sitzmann & Weinhardt, 2018). As long as satisfaction alone stands in for impact, organizations may learn to value the most comfortable course as the best one.

## 04 · Check What Changes after Training

**1. Before training, identify the behaviors that should change.** Agree with participants' managers on one or two observable behaviors, such as adding at least three lines of reasoning to a report or explicitly asking for an opposing view in a meeting.

**2. Immediately afterward, change the evaluation questions.** In place of relying solely on a five-point satisfaction scale, ask:

- What will you apply in the next two weeks, and in which task?
- Which question was most uncomfortable or made you think most deeply?
- What conditions might prevent you from putting the learning into practice?

**3. Assess learning directly.** Use the final twenty minutes for a task requiring participants to explain a key judgment without AI. Record whether they cannot explain it, can explain part of it, or can explain the supporting reasoning.

**4. Revisit the agreed behaviors with managers after 30 and 90 days.** Use a brief checklist, and ask participants for one work output demonstrating the behavior. HRD can report the proportion applying it.

**5. At 90 days, interview three strong-change and three no-change cases.** In twenty-minute interviews, ask what helped and what got in the way. Replace the satisfaction table in the management report with four fields: target behavior, application rate, a representative success case, and barriers with next actions.

### Insight Questions

1. Which of the indicators we report to management shows behavior change?
2. Have we had a poorly rated course that changed workplace practice? How did we evaluate it?
3. In making training easier with AI, are we also removing the productive difficulty that makes people think?
